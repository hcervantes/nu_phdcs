## Part 1 — Study Plan (6 Days, Oct 5–11)

### Day 1 (Mon, Oct 5) — Understand the Assignment &amp; Core Concepts

- Read the Lesson 7 introduction and Assignment 7 instructions carefully; map each rubric criterion to a deliverable.
- Master key definitions: AI ("intelligence demonstrated by machines" emulating cognitive functions), artificial neural networks (ANNs — interconnected nodes modeled on neurons/synapses, detecting hidden patterns and improving with learning), affective computing / Emotion AI.
- **Resources:** Lesson 7 document; Course textbook readings for the week.

### Day 2 (Tue, Oct 6) — Artificial Intelligence &amp; Anthropomorphism

- Study why researchers elevate anthropomorphism (ascribing human traits to AI) and its ethical concerns: deception, nefarious intent, loss of human control (Salles et al., 2020).
- Study the levels of anthropomorphism → self-congruence → self-AI integration framework, including moderating factors and individual/group/societal consequences (Alabed et al., 2022).
- Take notes on examples: social assistive robots (SARs) for Alzheimer's patients, gendered voice assistants (Alexa, Siri), companion chatbots (Replika, Woebot).
- **Resources:** Lesson 7 (AI section); Alabed et al. (2022).

### Day 3 (Wed, Oct 7) — Neural Networks &amp; Machine Learning Techniques

- Understand ANN structure (input → hidden → output layers, weighted connections, activation) and the analogy to neuroplasticity in the human brain.
- Compare the four ML techniques with examples:
  - **Supervised** — labeled data (e.g., fraud detection, medical diagnosis)
  - **Unsupervised** — unlabeled data, clustering (e.g., customer segmentation)
  - **Reinforcement** — reward-based trial and error (e.g., game-playing AI like OpenAI Five)
  - **Semi-supervised** — small labeled + large unlabeled datasets (e.g., image recognition with limited labels)
- See how RNNs predict sequential human decision-making (Lin et al., 2022) and how intelligent algorithms apply to real domains like home healthcare (Torabzadeh et al., 2022).
- **Resources:** Lesson 7 (Neural Networks section); Lin et al. (2022); Torabzadeh et al. (2022); Sarker (2021).

### Day 4 (Thu, Oct 8) — Learning and Emotions / Emotion AI

- Study how emotions are defined in AI: affect detection and response, sentiment analysis (text), audio, video facial-expression analysis, and behavior/gait/physiological signals.
- Study the role of emotions in agent performance (intent, perception, cognition) and the rationale for emulating emotions; consider perception accuracy and consequences of robot-displayed emotions (e.g., perceived anger as threatening; overly positive chatbots as condescending).
- Study affect-sensitive intelligent tutoring systems supporting self-regulated learning (Channa et al., 2021) and social acceptance/ethics of care robots (Engel, 2022).
- **Resources:** Lesson 7 (Learning and Emotions section); Hortensius et al. (2018); Channa et al. (2021); Engel (2022).

### Day 5 (Fri, Oct 9) — Synthesis &amp; Diverse Perspectives

- Build a comparison table: each topic (AI, ANNs, ML, emotions) — core approach, key example, ethical/social tension, and future research direction.
- Identify contrasting viewpoints: anthropomorphism as beneficial (engagement, acceptance, caregiving) vs. risky (deception, privacy, digital dementia, over-reliance).
- Draft the neural network diagram (structure and flow: inputs → hidden layers → outputs, with feedback/learning loop).

### Day 6 (Sat, Oct 10) — Build &amp; Record; Sunday buffer

- Build the 12–15 slide deck (see outline below), write speaker notes (200–350 words/slide), record audio, verify APA references, run a Turnitin pre-check, and submit early. Use **Sunday as buffer** for review, not creation.
