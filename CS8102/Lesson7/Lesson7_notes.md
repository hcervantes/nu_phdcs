## **II. Anthropomorphism in Artificial Intelligence**
### **A. Definition and Cognitive Basis**
- Humans naturally attribute human‑like traits, intentions, and emotions to non‑human agents.  
- **Source:** Salles, Evers & Farisco (2020) — anthropomorphism arises from cognitive shortcuts and social expectations.

### **B. Psychological Drivers**
- Pattern recognition, social cognition, and emotional projection.  
- Anthropomorphism increases perceived trustworthiness and familiarity.

### **C. Risks and Misinterpretations**
- Over‑trusting AI systems  
- Misjudging capabilities  
- False expectations of agency or emotion  
- **Source:** Salles et al. (2020) — anthropomorphism can distort user expectations and create ethical hazards.

---

## **III. Anthropomorphism and Self–AI Integration**
### **A. Self‑Congruence Theory**
- Users perceive AI as aligned with their identity when it feels human‑like.  
- **Source:** Alabed, Javornik & Gregory‑Smith (2022) — anthropomorphism → self‑congruence → psychological integration.

### **B. Emotional Attachment and Identity Fusion**
- Users may begin to treat AI as a social partner.  
- Increased reliance and emotional dependency.

### **C. Societal Implications**
- Over‑reliance on AI systems  
- Blurred boundaries between human and machine roles  
- **Source:** Alabed et al. (2022) — highlights risks of identity integration and emotional entanglement.

---

## **IV. Emotion AI (Affective Computing)**
### **A. Definition and Scope**
- AI systems designed to detect, interpret, and respond to human emotional states.  
- Includes facial expression analysis, voice sentiment detection, physiological signals.

### **B. Human Perception of Artificial Emotion**
- Humans interpret robot emotional cues using similar neural pathways as human emotion recognition.  
- **Source:** Hortensius, Hekele & Cross (2018) — emotional cues from robots activate social‑emotional processing.

### **C. Emotion AI in Learning Environments**
- Emotion‑aware tutoring systems detect frustration, confusion, engagement.  
- **Source:** Channa et al. (2021) — affect detection improves self‑regulated learning outcomes.

### **D. Benefits**
- Personalized learning  
- Improved human‑AI collaboration  
- Enhanced user experience

---

## **V. Ethical Tensions**
### **A. Deception and Misleading Cues**
- Anthropomorphic design can intentionally or unintentionally deceive users.  
- **Source:** Salles et al. (2020) — warns of ethical risks when AI appears more capable or emotional than it is.

### **B. Privacy and Emotional Surveillance**
- Emotion AI requires sensitive biometric and behavioral data.  
- **Source:** Channa et al. (2021) — emotion detection raises privacy concerns in educational settings.

### **C. Emotional Manipulation**
- AI systems may influence user emotions, decisions, or behaviors.  
- **Source:** Hortensius et al. (2018) — emotional cues can shape user perceptions and responses.

### **D. Over‑Reliance and Identity Integration**
- Users may form unhealthy attachments or dependencies.  
- **Source:** Alabed et al. (2022) — psychological integration can lead to over‑trust and blurred boundaries.
